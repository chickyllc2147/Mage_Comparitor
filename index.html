How this model was built

Every figure on this page comes from in-game DPM measurements taken by the YunaMS test team, fitted to a quadratic surface. Nothing here is derived from server formulas or datamining — it is empirical.

Buff conditions

All tests were run with the @buffme command applied, giving full mage buffs plus typical party buffs. The reasoning is simple: on this server, mages very rarely boss or grind without a full party buff stack, so testing unbuffed would produce numbers nobody actually plays at. Buffed conditions are the realistic baseline.

Eleven of these twelve buffs behave identically for both classes. Spell Booster — highlighted in the grid above — is the exception. It is covered at the end of this page.

Two buffs were deliberately left out

Why these INT and MAGIC ATK bounds

The tested range was chosen to span the full realistic spread of players on the server, so the model interpolates across builds people actually have rather than hypothetical ones.

BoundINTMAGIC ATKRepresents
Lower1000150Baseline stats of a fresh level 200 character
Upper37001700Current stats of the top-geared mages on the server

Anything inside those bounds is interpolation and well-supported by data. Anything outside is extrapolation — the sliders are capped accordingly, since a quadratic fit pushed beyond its sampled range can drift badly.

Why a 5×5 matrix, and why not assume linear scaling

Each class was tested at 5 INT levels × 8 MAGIC ATK levels = 40 combinations, repeated for each target count. The grid size was chosen deliberately:

  • Linear scaling cannot be assumed. MapleStory damage formulas multiply stat terms together, so DPM is expected to curve rather than rise in a straight line — and the interaction between INT and MAGIC ATK means the value of one stat depends on how much of the other you already have. A linear model would systematically misprice stats at the ends of the range.
  • Three points per axis would be insufficient. Three points define a parabola exactly, which means the fit would be perfect by construction and would tell you nothing about whether the relationship is actually quadratic.
  • Dense sampling leaves room to verify. Fitting six coefficients to 40 measurements leaves 34 degrees of freedom, so the residuals are meaningful. Fits land between R² 0.9959 and 0.99995, which supports the quadratic form describing the data rather than merely accommodating it.

Model form

Each class and target count is fitted independently to:

DPM = a + b·INT + c·INT² + d·MATK + e·MATK² + f·(INT×MATK)

The f term is what makes this a true surface rather than two independent curves — it captures how much each point of INT is worth given your current MAGIC ATK, and vice versa. That is also why the marginal DPM values shown above change as you move the sliders.

Notes specific to Blaze Wizard

  • B/W was tested with the Flame Vortex + Dragon Blaze skill macro at every target count, rather than a single skill, so its numbers reflect the macro as actually played.
  • Megiddo Flame was not used at any point in B/W testing. The B/W figures therefore reflect the Flame Vortex + Dragon Blaze macro alone, and would understate a rotation that includes it.
  • The 8-target grid is extrapolated, not measured. It is the 6-target grid scaled by 8/6 throughout, which assumes DPM continues to scale linearly with target count past 6. That assumption is consistent with the measured 5-to-6 step, but it has not been verified at 8. Because neither I/L nor F/P was tested past 6 targets, selecting 8 compares B/W's extrapolated 8-target output against those two at 6 targets — their maximum measured output — rather than a matched target count.
  • Five readings across the B/W grids were inconsistent enough with their neighbours to be excluded from the fits: INT 1000 / MAGIC ATK 967 at 1 target; INT 3700 / MAGIC ATK 1333 and INT 3025 / MAGIC ATK 150 at 5 targets; INT 1675 / MAGIC ATK 1700 and INT 3025 / MAGIC ATK 150 at 6 targets. Two of those broke monotonicity outright, recording less damage at higher MAGIC ATK than at lower. Re-testing those five combinations would tighten the B/W surfaces considerably.

Precision / Error

  • Fit quality varies by class. I/L and F/P sit at R² 0.9977 to 0.99995 with worst-case error of 1.5–3.9%. B/W is looser, R² 0.9959 to 0.9999 with worst-case error up to 8.9%, so small B/W gaps should be read with more caution than I/L or F/P ones.
  • Because all three classes were measured under the same conditions with the same methodology, the relative gaps are more reliable than any single absolute DPM figure.
  • A measurement uncertainty of ±1 MAGIC ATK translates to roughly ±0.03% to ±0.20% DPM, depending on where you sit in the range. The error is largest at the low end (INT 1000 / MAGIC ATK 150, about ±0.20%) and smallest at the high end (INT 3700 / MAGIC ATK 1700, about ±0.03%), since one point of MAGIC ATK is a smaller share of a larger total. This is well below the class gaps being reported, so a single point of MAGIC ATK drift does not change any conclusion here.
